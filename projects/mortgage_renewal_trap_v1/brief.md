# The Mortgage Renewal Trap

## Working Title

Your Mortgage Payment Can Rise Even When Rates Fall

## Promise

Explain why a Canadian mortgage payment can rise at renewal even if today's rates are lower than recent peaks.

## Target Viewer

Canadian homeowners, first-time buyers, and renters watching the housing market who want a plain-English explanation without panic.

## Core Tension

The headline says rates are coming down, but the borrower renews against the very low rate they signed years ago. The viewer expects relief, but the renewal can still feel like a payment shock.

## Story Shape

1. Open with the contradiction: lower rates, higher payments.
2. Explain the benchmark problem: renewal compares today's rate to the old contract rate.
3. Give the human consequence: a monthly squeeze, not necessarily a crash.
4. Add the caveat: risk is uneven by mortgage type, borrower group, and region.
5. End with the practical mental model: plan around the payment, not the headline rate.

## Source Notes

- CMHC says more than 1.5 million households had already renewed at higher rates by February 2026, with another million set to renew in the coming year.
- CMHC says arrears have been rising, but remain historically low, and pressure is concentrated in places such as Toronto and Vancouver and among pandemic-era, highly leveraged buyers.
- Bank of Canada analysis says many borrowers renewing in 2025 and 2026 are expected to face higher payments, with five-year fixed-rate borrowers renewing in 2026 among the most exposed.
- Bank of Canada Financial Stability Report 2026 says the last of the five-year, fixed-payment pandemic mortgages renew over the next 12 months and represent about 12% of all outstanding Canadian mortgages.
