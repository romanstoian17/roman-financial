If you live in Canada, the hard part is not finding an account. It is choosing which one gets your next dollar. Here is the simple version.

First, keep a small emergency buffer, because investments are not very helpful if one surprise bill forces you into debt.

After that, if you are saving for a first home, the FHSA deserves serious attention. It can give you a tax deduction going in, and tax-free growth when the money is used for a qualifying first home.

If your employer matches an RRSP contribution, that match usually jumps near the front of the line. Free money is not always free, but it is usually worth reading the plan carefully.

Then compare RRSP and TFSA. RRSPs can help more when your tax rate is high today. TFSAs are more flexible because withdrawals are tax-free and the room generally comes back later.

So the order is not magic. Buffer first. First-home money may point to the FHSA. Employer match can move the RRSP up. Flexible long-term money often fits the TFSA.

The best account is not the fanciest one. It is the one that matches the job your next dollar needs to do.
