In 2022, we came to Canada from Ukraine with two kids.

Almost all the money we had went into an old car.

We were renting. We had left our apartment back home. And when I saw houses selling for eight hundred thousand, nine hundred thousand, even a million dollars, I remember thinking, this is not difficult.

This is impossible.

But after the panic passed, my wife and I made one decision.

Maybe it is impossible. But let's still try.

Back in Ukraine, we had never really invested. One year before moving, I had only a couple thousand dollars to experiment with. That was it. So in Canada, I started researching like someone who was already late.

I learned about brokers. Employer matching. Cash-back accounts. Credit. Taxes. And then I found something important.

You do not need to be a professional investor to begin.

There are simple tools like ETFs, which are basically baskets of stocks. And for a beginner, that idea changed everything.

After rent, insurance, the car, kids, groceries, and all the normal bills, we did not have some huge amount left over. But we decided to invest one hundred and fifty dollars a week.

That was less than I could make in a day.

At the beginning, it felt almost pointless. You put money in, and the number still looks small. Nothing dramatic happens. Nobody claps for you. Your life looks exactly the same.

But then time starts doing its quiet work.

I was interested in investing, so I picked individual stocks for my own accounts. My wife was not interested in stock picking, so for her accounts, I chose the simpler ETF path.

Four years later, my accounts are around fifty eight thousand six hundred dollars. Her accounts are around fifty four thousand five hundred.

Together, that is about one hundred and thirteen thousand dollars.

And recently, we saw a similar house near the one we rent listed around five hundred twenty seven thousand dollars.

For the first time, the question was not, how could we ever buy a home?

The question became, should we buy one now, or keep growing?

That is the part I did not understand at the beginning.

The first financial goal after immigration is not getting rich. It is getting stable enough to have options.

Because once you have capital, time starts working differently. A broker matters. Taxes matter. Cashback matters. Employer match matters. Small decisions stop being small.

This channel is about that journey.

Money after immigration. The mistakes, the tools, and the systems that helped us go from zero to options.
