# Story Topic Candidates - 2026-08-24

Goal: choose Roman Financial episodes that feel like a story, not a list of facts. The desired pattern is closer to a measured explainer: viewer question, tension, mechanism, example, caveat, takeaway.

## Selection Criteria

- A normal viewer can feel the problem in real life.
- The topic has tension or a surprising contradiction.
- The script can use one clear visual metaphor.
- The episode can explain a mechanism, not just recite rules.
- The topic can be verified with current sources before publishing.

## Strongest Topic Candidates

### 1. The Mortgage Renewal Trap

Working title:

`Your Mortgage Payment Can Rise Even When Rates Fall`

Core question:

If interest rates are coming down, why are some Canadians still renewing into higher payments?

Why it works:

- Strong tension: lower rates, but higher payments.
- Clear Canadian relevance.
- Easy visual metaphor: old low-rate bridge leading into a higher toll booth.
- It feels like an Andrew Chang-style explainer: apparent contradiction -> mechanism -> consequence.

Possible 60-second shape:

1. Rates can fall, but your payment can still rise.
2. Many mortgages were signed when money was unusually cheap.
3. Renewal compares your old rate to today's rate, not last month's rate.
4. The shock is concentrated in specific borrower groups and regions.
5. Lower rates help, but they do not erase the reset.
6. Takeaway: plan around the payment, not the headline rate.

Sources to verify:

- CMHC mortgage renewal wave: https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2026/mortgage-renewal-wave-strains-some-regions-borrowers
- Bank of Canada mortgage renewal analysis: https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2025/07/staff-analytical-note-2025-21/
- About That reference topic: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1kGxZJwjt1A

### 2. The Silent Debt Warning Before Mortgage Trouble

Working title:

`The First Missed Payment Is Usually Not the Mortgage`

Core question:

What usually shows financial stress before someone misses a mortgage payment?

Why it works:

- Has a detective-story structure.
- Less obvious than another mortgage-rate video.
- Strong visual metaphor: small warning lights on credit cards, car loans, and lines of credit before the house alarm rings.
- Useful but not fearmongering.

Possible 60-second shape:

1. Mortgage trouble usually has early warning signs.
2. The mortgage is often the last bill people stop paying.
3. Stress can show up first in credit cards, auto loans, or lines of credit.
4. That matters because small missed payments can become a bigger budget problem.
5. Caveat: not every late payment means disaster.
6. Takeaway: watch the smaller leaks before the roof fails.

Sources to verify:

- Bank of Canada financial stability indicators: https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/indicators/financial-stability-indicators/
- Bank of Canada household section, Financial Stability Report 2026: https://www.bankofcanada.ca/publications/financial-stability-report/financial-stability-report-2026/households/
- Bank of Canada early warning article: https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2026/02/sparks-at-bank-article-2026-7/

### 3. Your Money Is Losing Weight

Working title:

`Your Money Is Not Disappearing. It Is Shrinking.`

Core question:

Why can money feel weaker even when your bank balance is the same?

Why it works:

- Very Andrei-style: personal, simple, slightly philosophical.
- Easy to understand and visualize.
- Can connect inflation, cash, savings accounts, and investing without becoming a lecture.
- Strong metaphor: a dollar bill slowly losing mass on a scale.

Possible 60-second shape:

1. Your balance can stay the same while your buying power falls.
2. Inflation is not just higher prices; it is a smaller unit of money.
3. Cash is useful for safety, but weak as a long-term storage tool.
4. The point is not to panic-invest.
5. Caveat: emergency money has a different job.
6. Takeaway: every dollar needs a job: spend, protect, or grow.

Sources to verify:

- Statistics Canada recent economy summary: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2026004/article/00005-eng.htm
- Statistics Canada household balance sheet: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260612/dq260612a-eng.htm
- Andrei Jikh reference topic: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nHy5u06we_s

### 4. The Grocery Bill Is a Debt Story Now

Working title:

`When Groceries Move From Budget Problem To Debt Problem`

Core question:

What changes when regular expenses start going on credit?

Why it works:

- Very relatable.
- More emotional than tax-account content.
- Clear mechanism: temporary cash-flow bridge can become permanent interest expense.
- Strong visual metaphor: grocery cart rolling onto a credit-card conveyor belt.

Possible 60-second shape:

1. Groceries are not optional, which is why this is different.
2. Credit can smooth timing, but interest changes the price.
3. The risk is when regular expenses become revolving debt.
4. One late balance can make next month's groceries more expensive.
5. Caveat: credit cards are not the villain; carrying balances is the problem.
6. Takeaway: separate payment method from payment plan.

Sources to verify:

- FCAC debt and regular-expense borrowing notes: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/corporate/transparency/briefing-packages-parliamentary-committee-appearances/standing-committee-finance-april-13-2026.html
- Bank of Canada household stress indicators: https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/indicators/financial-stability-indicators/
- Statistics Canada inflation/economy updates: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2026004/article/00005-eng.htm

### 5. Why Renting Can Feel Like Losing, Even When It Is Rational

Working title:

`Renting Is Not Throwing Money Away. But It Can Still Trap You.`

Core question:

Why does renting feel financially wrong even when buying is not affordable?

Why it works:

- Emotionally sticky.
- Avoids boring rent-vs-buy calculators.
- Allows a balanced caveat, which builds trust.
- Visual metaphor: two ladders, one toward ownership and one toward flexibility, both missing some rungs.

Possible 60-second shape:

1. Renting feels like paying for someone else's asset.
2. Buying feels like progress, but the entry fee changed.
3. The real comparison is not rent versus mortgage; it is flexibility versus forced savings plus risk.
4. High prices can make the math less obvious.
5. Caveat: local market and life plans matter.
6. Takeaway: housing is not just an investment. It is also a liability and a life decision.

Sources to verify:

- CMHC housing and mortgage research: https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2026/mortgage-renewal-wave-strains-some-regions-borrowers
- Statistics Canada household finances: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260612/dq260612a-eng.htm

### 6. The Employer Match Is Not Just Free Money

Working title:

`Free Money Usually Has Fine Print`

Core question:

Why is employer RRSP matching powerful, and what should someone check before treating it as free money?

Why it works:

- Directly useful.
- Has a caveat and mechanism.
- Strong visual metaphor: two coins magnetically snapping together, with a small rulebook underneath.

Possible 60-second shape:

1. An employer match can be one of the fastest returns you get.
2. But the plan rules matter.
3. Vesting, fees, investment choices, and contribution limits can change the real value.
4. The match may still be worth it, but read the plan.
5. Takeaway: free money is worth checking, not ignoring.

Sources to verify:

- CRA RRSP rules: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans.html

### 7. The Payment You Can Afford Is Not the Same as the Price You Can Afford

Working title:

`Monthly Payments Hide The Real Price`

Core question:

Why do cars, phones, furniture, and loans feel affordable until the total cost appears?

Why it works:

- Broad audience.
- Good for a 60-second visual explainer.
- Easy metaphor: price tag split into tiny monthly slices that reassemble into a bigger number.

Possible 60-second shape:

1. Monthly payments make expensive things feel smaller.
2. That is useful when cash flow matters.
3. But it can hide interest, fees, and total cost.
4. The danger is stacking too many small payments.
5. Caveat: financing is not always bad.
6. Takeaway: judge the total cost before judging the payment.

Sources to verify:

- FCAC borrowing guidance and debt data: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency.html

### 8. The Cash Cushion Nobody Wants To Build

Working title:

`The Most Boring Money Move Is Often The First One`

Core question:

Why does an emergency fund matter before investing?

Why it works:

- Simple but still emotional.
- Strong visual metaphor: small boring umbrella over a much larger investment plant.
- Good evergreen starter topic.

Possible 60-second shape:

1. An emergency fund feels slow because it does not grow dramatically.
2. But it prevents forced bad decisions.
3. Without cash, a surprise bill can push you into debt or force an investment sale.
4. The job of this money is not growth. It is protection.
5. Takeaway: boring money can protect exciting money.

Sources to verify:

- FCAC budgeting/emergency fund guidance: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency.html
