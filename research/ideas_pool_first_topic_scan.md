## Best First Topic Candidates

### 1. Pay Off Debt or Invest First?

Why it fits:

- Sits between credit/debt and beginner investing.
- Easy to explain with simple numbers.
- Evergreen and safe for a first Roman Financial video.
- Lets Roman feel practical, friendly, and useful rather than alarmist.

Recommended format:

- 75-90 second Short.
- Simple example: credit card at high interest versus broad-market investing assumption.
- Rule of thumb: emergency buffer first, high-interest debt next, investing after that.
- Avoid personalized advice language.

### 2. The Best Order To Invest Your Money

Why it fits:

- Matches recent reference-pool interest in investing order and retirement accounts.
- Can become a channel pillar.
- Works for Canadian or general audience versions.

Risk:

- Account order changes by country, so we need to decide Canada-first, U.S.-first, or general framework.

### 3. Are You Really Middle Class?

Why it fits:

- Current competitor titles suggest strong audience pull.
- Good for relatable storytelling.
- Easy to visualize with rent, groceries, car payment, debt, savings, and income.

Risk:

- Needs fresh country-specific numbers if we make concrete claims.

### 4. Renting vs Buying: The Real Math

Why it fits:

- Strong evergreen finance topic.
- Visual and easy to compare.
- Good for Roman's calm explainer identity.

Risk:

- More complex than the first topic; likely better as episode 2 or 3.

### 5. How To Invest Your First $1,000

Why it fits:

- Beginner-friendly and clickable.
- Works well with simple visual steps.

Risk:

- Can drift toward advice if phrased too personally. Keep it educational and example-based.

## Recommended First Topic

Start with **Pay Off Debt or Invest First?**

Reason:

It is the strongest first-test topic because it validates the full workflow without requiring recent market data, complicated tax/account details, or a country-specific calculator. It also lets us introduce Roman's personality: calm, public-friendly, practical, and not hype-driven.

- Should You Invest While You Still Have Debt?

## First Video Angle

Premise:

> If your debt interest rate is higher than what your investments can reasonably earn, investing first can feel productive while quietly costing you money.

Simple structure:

1. Hook: "Investing while carrying bad debt can be like filling a bucket with a hole in it."
2. Context: A person has $1,000 and credit card debt.
3. Compare: 20% debt interest versus a possible investment return.
4. Framework: emergency buffer, high-interest debt, employer match if relevant, then investing.
5. Takeaway: money order matters.
