# Roman Financial Speaking Style

This is a reference for script writing and voiceover direction. Use these as high-level craft patterns, not direct imitation of any creator.

## Reference Signals

### About That with Andrew Chang

Useful traits:

- Starts with a public question or confusing headline, then slows down to explain why it matters.
- Uses an investigative rhythm: claim, context, consequence, caveat.
- Feels conversational, but still fact-checked and structured.
- Lets the viewer feel oriented before adding complexity.
- Uses transitions such as: "Here's why that matters", "The important part is", "But there is a catch", "So what does that actually mean?"

Use for Roman Financial:

- Open with a practical question a Canadian viewer may already have.
- Explain the mechanism before giving the takeaway.
- Include one caveat when the topic could be oversimplified.
- Avoid sounding like a list of tips. Build a small argument.

### Andrei Jikh

Useful traits:

- Uses curiosity and personal framing before the technical explanation.
- Avoids sounding rushed; important ideas get a beat to land.
- Mixes numbers with simple analogies and light skepticism.
- Often makes finance feel less intimidating by saying the quiet part plainly.
- Uses visual metaphors and simple mental models instead of dense terminology.

Use for Roman Financial:

- Add one human sentence before the facts.
- Use a clean metaphor for every technical concept.
- Keep the tone lightly skeptical of hype, but not cynical.
- Let the voice smile slightly; not comedian energy, not banker energy.

## Roman Financial Voice

Roman should sound like:

- A calm friend who read the boring document so the viewer does not have to.
- Curious, practical, and mildly skeptical.
- Clear enough for beginners, but not childish.
- Canadian when relevant, but not forced.

Roman should not sound like:

- A pure news anchor.
- A hype finance influencer.
- A spreadsheet lecture.
- A direct copy of any reference creator.

## Script Formula

Each short should usually follow:

1. **Question:** What is the viewer trying to decide?
2. **Tension:** Why the obvious answer is incomplete.
3. **Mechanism:** The simple rule or framework.
4. **Example:** A small concrete situation.
5. **Caveat:** When the rule changes.
6. **Takeaway:** The clean decision sentence.

## Sentence Rules

- Use mostly 7-14 word sentences.
- Keep one idea per sentence.
- Put pauses after important turns with punctuation, not filler.
- Avoid long stacked clauses.
- Use plain words first, technical terms second.
- Every 20-30 seconds, include a "why this matters" line.

## Voiceover Rules

- Target a measured pace, not fast delivery.
- Default Fish speed: `0.92`.
- Prefer punctuation that creates natural breath:
  - Periods for decisive ideas.
  - Commas for small turns.
  - Short paragraph breaks between scene chunks.
- Avoid dense number strings in narration. Put precise figures in subtitles or visual overlays when needed.

## Subtitle Rules

- Show one stable phrase or sentence page at a time.
- Highlight the spoken word inside that stable page.
- Do not roll the word window continuously while the sentence is still being spoken.
- Split long narration into subtitle pages of roughly 8-12 words.

## Quick Rewrite Checklist

Before rendering, ask:

- Is there a real viewer question in the first 10 seconds?
- Did we explain why the topic matters before listing facts?
- Is there at least one analogy or visual mental model?
- Is there one caveat so the video does not overpromise?
- Could the same script be spoken comfortably without rushing?
